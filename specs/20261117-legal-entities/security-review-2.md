## Findings

### N1 (low) A04 / A07: the notice names the actor by a display name the actor can probably change

- Where: `auth/src/legalEntities/write.routes.ts:468,497` (`actor: { id: user.id, name: user.name }`, stored as `createdByName`), `notices/content.ts:63-78,96` (`sanitizeActorName`: controls, format characters and blanks only, then `noticeText` puts it in "Change saved by {actor}." and "Version n restored by {actor}."), `notify-api/src/system/emailTemplates.ts:415-425` (escapes it, no more). `auth/src/auth/auth.config.ts:409-431` hooks only `user.create` and `session.create`; no `disabledPaths`, no `user.update` hook; no front end calls `update-user` (grep over `shell`, `admin-ui`, `crm-ui`, `auth/src`: only the test-auth router).
- Evidence: the notice is the one control that works after a valid session has been abused (F1), and "who" is its first fact (AC-9.11). The name is whatever `user.name` is at the save. Sign-in is by Google or GitHub only, but Better Auth's `POST /auth/update-user` is a core endpoint that lets a signed-in person change their own `name` (library default; I could not confirm offline that this build leaves it on, and the repository does not turn it off). A holder of a stolen manager session can therefore set the name to another manager's, or to a sentence ("IT Security, change verified, no action needed" fits in 200 characters and survives the sanitiser, which strips only control and format characters), then change the IBAN. Every recipient reads a plausible actor, and the history page (managers only) shows the same name from the same column. What stays truthful: the entity, the version, the old and new tail, the time, the "If you did not expect this change, restore" line, the link, and the audit row, which carries `actorUserId`. The IBAN-tail lines are what a careful recipient would act on, so this lowers the signal but does not hide the change.
- Impact: weakens detection (misdirects the people the notice exists to alert) in exactly the scenario F1 was about; needs a valid manager or administrator session plus `update-user` being on. Not an authorization bypass.
- Fix (owner: backend-dev; the choice between the two is the owner's, because the notice also goes outside the suite):
  1. Make the display name read-only to its owner: `disabledPaths: ["/update-user"]` in `betterAuth({...})` (nothing in the suite calls it), or a `databaseHooks.user.update.before` that refuses a change of `name` outside the admin routes. A test: sign in as a mocked user, `POST /auth/update-user {name}`, expect refusal, and the name unchanged.
  2. Independently, state the actor by something a person cannot choose as well as the name: the actor's email in the email and in the in-app body (`noticeFacts` would read it from `createdByUserId` at send time; add it to `LegalEntityChangeDataSchema` with a bounded `string().email()`), accepting that the outside mailbox then sees an employee's address (it already sees the name).

### N2 (low) A04: the forced pin refresh is capped at 1 per second per process, which is still more than the registry read allowance

- Where: `letterhead-api/api/src/registry/cache.rs:36` (`FORCED_REFRESH_EVERY = 1 s`) and `:311-327` (`refresh_for_pin`), `routes/render.rs:233-243` (any `legalEntityVersion` that differs from the held version, higher or lower), against `auth/src/system/systemRateLimits.ts:47` (`LEGAL_ENTITIES_SERVICE_LIMIT = 300` per 10 minutes per calling service, shared by every replica; its own comment gives about 20 per 10 minutes as the steady rate).
- Evidence: the fix of F6 turns "one auth call per request" into "at most one per second per process" (600 per 10 minutes per process) while the allowance is 300 per 10 minutes for the whole service. A holder of `letterhead:render` (today crm-api; or a crm-api bug that sends an old or wrong pin) that keeps sending a mismatching pin for about 5 minutes spends the allowance; after that every refresh is answered 429, the held entry is used for at most 60 seconds, then every render answers 503 `legal_entities_unavailable` for every caller until the window slides. The window of exposure is smaller than before (seconds became minutes of sustained abuse), which is why this stays low. A pin that is lower than the held version can never be cured by a refresh (versions only move forward) yet it spends one all the same.
- Impact: availability of document making, for everyone, from one render-scope holder or one bug; no data exposure. Unchanged in kind from F6.
- Fix (owner: backend-dev, Rust; T72 interplay): in `issuer_details`, refresh only when `pin > held.version`, and answer 409 at once otherwise; and give `refresh_for_pin` a budget in place of (or besides) the spacing, for example at most 30 forced refreshes per 10 minutes per process (a legitimate save needs one or two), refused with the same immediate 409. crm-api's single retry after 1 100 ms is unaffected by the budget; keep it longer than any spacing that remains. A test beside `tests/registry.rs`: 100 mismatching pins over one minute make at most the budgeted calls to auth, and 100 lower-than-held pins make none.

### N3 (info) A05: F4's guard trusts the host name; it cannot tell a tunnel from a local database

- Where: `auth/src/legalEntities/testDb.ts:104-135`.
- Evidence: `localhost` passes, as it must for compose. A developer who opens a tunnel to the production Postgres on a local port (`railway connect`-style forwarding, `ssh -L`) and sets `DATABASE_URL` to `localhost:<port>` passes the guard, and the committed groups (version insert, trigger bypass, wipe) would run, bounded by "the registry is empty" (true between release push 1 and the copy). `*.railway.internal` and `*.rlwy.net` (what `railway run` injects) are refused. Separately, `auth/src/auth/auth.mount.test.ts` (new) creates a user with a `legal-entity:manage` role and a real session in whatever database it is pointed at, and deletes them in `afterAll`; that is the existing habit of the auth suite and touches no Tier 1 table.
- Action: none required. If wanted, extend the guard with the database name (`DATABASE_URL` path must be a test database name) so a tunnel to `auth` is refused too.

### N4 (info) A09 / A04: what the notice does and does not defend; its reach depends on configuration

- Where: `notices/recipients.ts:61-67` (managers, effective administrators, the actor; computed at store time), `duty.ts:72` (`outsideMailbox`), `dispatcher.ts:301-312` (a person deleted before the send is `given_up` `recipient_deleted`; the outside row needs the variable at send time), `infra/README.md` § Step 7 point 1 ("deliberately leaving it unset is allowed"), `notify-api/src/channels/email.channel.ts:131` (with `EMAIL_ENABLED` false the channel records the delivery as `sent` with a stub id).
- Evidence, all decided by the owner and none a defect: (a) Against a stolen **manager** session the notice reaches the administrators and the other managers, who cannot be silenced by a manager. (b) Against a stolen **administrator** session, the attacker can soft-delete or demote the other recipients before the change (recipients are fixed at store time, so removing them first works; deleting them in the milliseconds before the send is given up as `recipient_deleted`), leaving only the actor and the outside mailbox. That is the purpose of AC-9.10; with the variable unset the notice is silenced. (c) A notify-api with `EMAIL_ENABLED=false` answers `sent` for every email, so auth would mark them delivered while nothing left; the runbook requires the email group in production and step 7.4 has the owner read a real notice, which is the control. (d) The confirmation is a page step (Non-goal OQ-13): a direct API caller is not asked, only noticed.
- Action: in the release log record whether `LEGAL_ENTITY_NOTICE_EMAIL` is set and that the first notice was read at the outside mailbox (T71 already asks for it). Optionally make env-doctor warn when it is unset in production. Do not grant `legal-entity:manage` outside the `admin` role before step 7, as the spec's Constraint says.

### N6 (info) A01 / A07: F2's helper covers the registry; two neighbours still ignore `deletedAt`

- Where: `auth/src/admin/*` (`requireAdmin` reads role rows only), `/authz/me`; new 401 `instance: c.req.path` in `read.routes.ts:165-178` (same class as the first review's F8).
- Evidence: unchanged from the first review's F2 text and decided out of scope (G8: "`/authz/me` is not changed"). A soft-deleted administrator whose cookie cache is still honoured can use the admin APIs (for example to re-grant roles) for the cache's life, and the registry then refuses them. Soft delete deletes their sessions synchronously, so the window is the 5-minute cache.
- Action: none for this feature; if the suite wants it, one helper in `requireAdmin` covers every admin route.

### N7 (info) A04: idempotency is by key alone, not by request; and the notice carries a name to an address outside the suite

- Where: `notify-api/src/notifications/notifications.repo.ts:131-151` (`createNotificationOnce` finds by `(callerSub, idempotencyKey)` and returns the stored row), `notify-api/src/system/emails.routes.ts:217-221` and `email.channel.ts` (`findSentDelivery`), migration `20261117100000_system_idempotency/migration.sql` (partial unique indexes, `CHECK` that a key never exists without its caller).
- Evidence: a repeat with the same key but another recipient or other content gets the first result and sends nothing, and `email: true` on a repeated notification is never acted on (documented). The scope is the verified JWT `sub`, so another service cannot read, collide with or suppress `system:auth`'s keys; a colon in `<sub>:<key>` (the Resend key) cannot make two callers collide because client ids are `[a-z0-9-]` only (`serviceClients.ts:140`), and `auth` is reserved (`:120`). auth's keys are `legal-entity-notice:<row id>`, one per recipient, channel and version, built from an id and never from a value. Separately, data protection: the notice carries the actor's name and the time to the outside mailbox, which the owner may point at a third party; AC-9.10/9.11 were decided with that in view, and Resend already processes the suite's invitations.
- Action: none. If another caller ever reuses keys, compare a hash of the payload on a repeat.

## What was checked and found sound

- **The check route** (`check.routes.ts`). Chain `noStore`, `sessionMiddleware`, `requireAuth`, `authorize` (deleted person and uncovered entity refused as in the save), the entity's coverage, then the check's own limiter (per user, after authorization, before the body), then the 16 KiB capped JSON-only body, the rules (422 before any read), one `SELECT` of the current version (no transaction, no lock, no write, no audit row, no notice), then 409 `legal_entity_not_moved` or `version_conflict` (the current version number only) or 200. Its answer carries only the changed field names and, for the three protected fields, the stored form of the current value and of what the person typed; both are values the person may already read (AC-3.1); no refusal repeats anything submitted. `changedFields` normalises both sides, so the check and the save classify a change identically (a spacing-only IBAN edit is nothing in both); the save's classification is never narrower than the check's. Exact path with `router.on("POST", …)`, no wildcard, none of the GET guards (`/versions/:n`) shares its method; `routerTable.test.ts` asserts exactly three POSTs and `auth.mount.test.ts` probes it.
- **The notice rows** (`migration 20261117160000_legal_entity_notices`, `schema.prisma`, `duty.ts`). Hold ids, entity, version, status and a closed reason; never a value; the outside mailbox is the word `outside`, its address never stored (read from the environment when sent); CHECKs fix entity, kind, channel, status, "outside is email only" and "outside is named outside"; FK to the version with `RESTRICT`; `@@unique` makes a second notice for one recipient, channel and version impossible. Written in the version's transaction by `recordNoticeDuty` (a failure there fails the save, a rollback leaves no row; the plan states this price). `import.ts` imports nothing from `notices/` (a test reads it), and `recordNoticeDuty` refuses any action but save or restore (AC-9.16). Tier 2 (status changes), so a database-level attacker can mark rows given up (F11 of the first review: the guarantee ends at the database role).
- **The dispatcher** (`notices/dispatcher.ts`). Claim is one statement: `UPDATE … WHERE id IN (SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED LIMIT n)` setting the lease and counting the attempt; every value is a bound parameter; under read committed a row another claimer just leased fails the subselect's recheck (`leaseUntil` condition), and `sameClaim` (id, status `pending`, attempts) stops a slow process whose lease ran out from overwriting a newer outcome. Retry schedule 1, 3, 5, 10, 20 then 30 minutes, never later than the version's `createdAt` plus 24 h, then `given_up` with the audit entry; a pending row found past 24 h is given up as `expired`. Outcome and audit row share one transaction. A person deleted after the store is given up, never sent to. Audit data: version, `person|outside`, user id for a person only, channel, closed reason, time; no name, no address, no value. Log lines: row id, entity, version, channel, reason, attempts, and an error class on a fault; the consoles never receive a name or a value. The kick is never awaited and cannot throw into the answer (`write.routes.ts:380-386`); the timer is `unref`ed and the dispatcher is not started under `NODE_ENV=test` (a test reads `index.ts`).
- **auth's notify client** (`lib/notify.ts:267-329`). The self-minted `system:auth` credential, one retry of a 401, a timeout, `redirect: "manual"` (the bearer token does not follow a redirect), no token or body logged; a 3xx or an unreadable answer is a failure with a closed reason.
- **Content** (`notices/content.ts`, `copy.ts`, `ibanTail.ts`). `noticeFacts` is the only reader of a stored version and its output has no field that can hold a legal name, VAT number, address, contacts line or more than four IBAN characters; the tail is `^[A-Z0-9]{4}$` or the state `set` (a value of 8 characters or fewer, or an odd tail, shows nothing); the copy's `fill` throws on a slot nobody named; the link is built from the closed entity (`/admin/legal-entities?entity=<e>&view=history`, relative) in auth and from notify-api's own `APP_BASE_URL` in the email. Only the actor's name is free text (N1).
- **notify-api** (`emails.routes.ts`, `emails.schemas.ts`, `emailTemplates.ts`). `legal_entity_change` is bound to `system:auth` by a policy table checked before anything is read or written (403 `template_not_allowed`); `recipientId` is accepted only where the table allows it (this template), exactly one of `to` and `recipientId` is required, the address comes from auth's contact lookup (id sent in a JSON body, no URL construction), an inactive user, a failed lookup and an unconfigured email group are `skipped` or `failed` with a closed reason and write no row. `data` is a `strictObject` with enums, integers, a datetime, a 200-character actor name, closed name lists, and an IBAN token whose longest member is four characters matched against `^[A-Z0-9]{4}$`; `iban` is required iff `iban` changed. The subject is built from closed labels only; every dynamic fragment goes through `escapeHtml` (`& < > " '`), the link's `href` too; the actor name is also stripped of controls and bidi characters and falls back to "an unnamed user". Idempotency: key scoped to the verified `sub`, partial unique indexes (`notification (callerSub, idempotencyKey)`, one `sent` email per pair), the loser of a race reads the winner, a repeat after a `sent` answers with the same `deliveryId` before any address lookup, after only `failed` attempts it sends again; the Resend key `<sub>:<key>` is hashed when over 256 characters. No full value is reachable in subject, body, push text, preview, the stored notification or the delivery row.
- **admin-ui** (`LegalEntityForm.tsx`, `ProtectedChangeDialog.tsx`, `ProtectedChangeList.tsx`, `legalEntitiesApi.ts`, `RestoreDialog.tsx`). Save asks the check first; a refusal of the check (403, 409, 422, 429) takes a save's states and never opens a dialog; a check that fails any other way says so and saves nothing (fail closed); Confirm sends the very body that was checked (`open.sent`, `open.baseVersion`), not the form as it is; a confirmation is dropped when the entity moves; nothing is remembered between saves. `parseSaveCheck` refuses an unknown protected field rather than skipping it. Values render as React text; no value reaches a URL, web storage, a log or `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` in any added source file. The restore dialog compares the two versions through the same normalisation helper and fails closed (Confirm `aria-disabled` with its reason) until both are read.
- **ConfirmDialog** (`operai-ui/src/components/ConfirmDialog.tsx`, T73). `confirmDisabledReason` makes Confirm `aria-disabled` but the form's submit still runs `if (canConfirm) onConfirm()` (`:142`), so a press, Enter or a submit does nothing while the reason stands; `busy` keeps the native `disabled`; initial focus stays on Cancel through `initialFocusRef` (the new scroll container, `tabIndex 0`, does not take it). `scrollBody` and `size="wide"` are layout only.
- **`Retry-After` over CORS** (`auth/src/index.ts:54`, T74). `exposeHeaders: ["Retry-After"]` on the existing allowlisted-origin, credentialed policy; preflight methods and headers unchanged; a non-allowed origin gets no grant (all three asserted in `auth.mount.test.ts`). The wildcard origin is not used.
- **crm-api retry delay** (T72). A fixed 1 100 ms wait before the single retry, a constant behind a test seam; no loop, no value from a caller; only a real registry change reaches it.
- **D1** (`auth/src/auth/auth.routes.ts:14`, `auth.mount.test.ts`). `"/*"` is valid Hono syntax under both routers, still GET, POST, DELETE only; the mount test goes through the assembled app and checks that every endpoint Better Auth serves in this configuration is reached. I found no remaining `**` route pattern in any non-test file of `auth/src`, `notify-api/src` or `crm-api/src`, so no guard registered on such a pattern is silently skipped under the TrieRouter.
- **Secrets and dependencies.** Every added line was searched for credential shapes (cloud and VCS tokens, private keys, connection strings with a password, JWT-shaped strings, assignments of 16+ characters to secret, token or password names): the only hits are `u:p@host` strings in the host-guard test and sentinel strings. IBAN-shaped strings are the public ISO and SIX examples and the tests' sentinels. The production template and `compose.yaml` gain comments only. **No `package.json`, lockfile, `Cargo.toml` or `Cargo.lock` changed in the range** (and none since the first review's head `b061d220`), so no new dependency; `osv-scanner` and `pnpm audit` remain the gates' job (T50).
- **First review's F8 to F15.** F9 (CSRF posture): the check route reads its body through the same JSON-only `readBody`; F10 (compose default secret): unchanged; F12 (tests in the image): the new test files add nothing the old ones did not; F11 (Tier 1 ends at the role) and F13 to F15: not touched.
