A change to a legal entity's name, VAT number or IBAN, by a save or a restore, now tells people. In the same step as the new version, auth records a notice for each active manager covering the entity, every effective administrator, the person who made the change, and an outside mailbox set by the new `LEGAL_ENTITY_NOTICE_EMAIL` (optional, email only, the address is read when sending and never stored); a change with nobody to tell is written to the audit log as `legal_entity.notice_no_recipients`. A dispatcher sends them right after the change and sweeps every 15 seconds on every replica: an in-app notification and the `legal_entity_change` email through notify-api (which must be released first), retried after 1, 3, 5, 10 and 20 minutes and then every 30, and given up 24 hours after the version was stored. The audit log shows the first failure, a later delivery and a give-up. A notice names the changed details and shows an IBAN by its last 4 characters only; the import never sends one. A notice identifies who made the change by the account email captured in the version's transaction and kept on the notice rows (a new `authorEmail` column on `legal_entity_notice`; the version table is untouched), never a display name: the in-app notification (en/it), the push and the email data (`actorEmail`) all carry it, and a null or malformed email reads "no email address on record". The email is cleared from a notice row once it reaches its terminal status (delivered or given up), enforced by the database (a trigger and a CHECK, migration `20261118120000_legal_entity_notice_author_email_clear`), so it is not kept after the notice is done. A holder cannot change their own account email (Better Auth refuses), so the address captured for a change stays the one on record for its author. The top-level error log carries only the error name and code. Release step 7: set `LEGAL_ENTITY_NOTICE_EMAIL`, confirm a notice arrives in production, and only then let anyone but the `admin` role manage a legal entity. The migration is additive.
